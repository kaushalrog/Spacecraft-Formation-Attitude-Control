Keeping four spacecraft pointed the same way when their actuators weaken

Team 14: C Rishitha, Y Karthikeya, Ashwin Tyagi and S Kaushal. School of Artificial Intelligence, Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Coimbatore.

In a formation mission, several spacecraft have to turn and hold the same attitude together. In practice their actuators lose effectiveness, torque is limited, there are disturbances, and the model of each spacecraft is never exact.

A standard controller recomputes and broadcasts its command at every time step, which costs communication and computation. Event-triggered control updates only when the error has grown enough to matter. Self-triggered control goes a step further and works out in advance when the next update will be needed, so it doesn't have to keep checking.

We reproduced the approach from Xie, Sheng and Chen's 2024 paper (reference 1 below) in MATLAB and Simulink. We built a four-spacecraft model and compared three ways of scheduling a fault-tolerant sliding-mode controller.

Open the interactive simulation

It shows the four spacecraft, their communication links and when each one updates. You can switch between the three cases, inject an actuator fault and watch the attitude errors settle.

What we built

  • A nonlinear attitude model for four spacecraft, using Modified Rodrigues Parameters. Each spacecraft has three attitude and three angular-velocity states, 24 in total.
  • A distributed sliding-mode controller. Each spacecraft uses its own error and its neighbours' errors through the communication graph.
  • Actuator effectiveness loss, time-varying disturbances, model uncertainty, and torque limited to ±0.2 N·m.
  • Three versions of the same controller. In case 1 the command updates at every time step. In case 2 it updates only when a dynamic trigger condition fires. In case 3 the next update time is computed ahead (self-triggered).

Kaushal built the Simulink model, set up the three triggering cases and produced the graphs and tables. Rishitha did the spacecraft dynamics and disturbances, Karthikeya the sliding-mode controller and fault tolerance, and Ashwin the literature review and mathematical formulation.

What we saw

All three cases bring every spacecraft to the shared attitude, and the angular-velocity errors settle as well. The torque commands stay inside the ±0.2 N·m limit throughout.

The differences are in how often the controller acts. In case 2 the torque switches back and forth more often, because the command only changes at trigger events, but it stays within the limit. The trigger plots show each spacecraft updating at its own irregular times, not on a fixed clock. That is what saves communication and computation compared with case 1.

All 14 figures (Fig02 to Fig15) are in the output_images folder. Running sff_run_all in MATLAB regenerates them.

What this doesn't cover yet

  • Everything is numerical simulation. There is no hardware-in-the-loop or experimental test.
  • Only four spacecraft. We haven't looked at how communication and computation grow with larger formations.
  • Faults are a fixed loss of actuator effectiveness. Multiple, time-varying or intermittent faults aren't modelled, and neither are communication delays or packet loss.

The next steps would be hardware-in-the-loop testing, larger formations, those richer fault and communication models, and adaptive tuning of the controller gains and trigger parameters.
